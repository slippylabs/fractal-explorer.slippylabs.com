Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and the URL follows you — every view is a link. Colouring is the continuous escape estimate, not the raw iteration count, which is why the bands have no stair-steps in them.

In Mandelbrot view, shift-click any point to use it as this c and jump to the Julia set it generates. Points just inside the Mandelbrot boundary make the most interesting ones.

Each pixel is one complex number c, and the only question asked of it is whether iterating z → z² + c from zero stays bounded. It either escapes past radius 2 — after which it is gone for good — or it never does. Black is “never did, within the iteration limit”, so raising that limit shrinks the black and reveals thinner filaments; the limit is the only thing between you and an exact answer.

Everything is computed in double, which runs out at roughly 1015× magnification — past that, neighbouring pixels round to the same complex number and the image goes blocky. Going deeper needs arbitrary-precision arithmetic with perturbation theory, which is a different program. The readout says when you are close.
